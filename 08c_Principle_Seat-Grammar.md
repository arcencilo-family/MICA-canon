# Chapter 08c — The seat grammar (how a human seat stays honest without a merit test)

**Covers:** the one grammar every human seat follows (taken, never imposed; no merit definition; one signed floor, the acknowledgment of sub-certainty; bound to diligence, never to outcomes), why the four clauses are one, and how it renders at the arbiter, doctor and co-operator seats.
**Status:** Principle chapter, global (all three operator seats). Two listed triples are one chapter by the arbiter's ruling, because the source states the four clauses as one posture. The co-operator rendering rests on a chapter that has not been through a review round.
**Source:** derived from the canon chapters it cites (07a, 07b, 07c, 11); no separate specification.
Claim tags: see 01.

---

Every human seat the architecture depends on is built from the same four clauses,
and the four are one grammar: remove any clause and the seat becomes either
untakeable by an honest party or invisible in its capture.

---

## 0. In brief

MICA's design leans on named humans — a doctor who attests, an arbiter who
resolves, a co-operator who receives the daily warning. Every one of these seats
follows the same four rules: the role is offered and accepted, never assigned to
someone (an imposed overseer defeats itself); there is deliberately no competence
test for it (any test would be gamed first by exactly the wrong people); the one
thing the holder must do is sign that they know no one can be certain here; and
they answer for doing the work diligently — never for failing to guarantee what
the architecture itself says nobody can guarantee. Together these rules make the
seat one an honest person can actually accept, and one whose capture by a
dishonest person leaves visible traces.

## 1. The one statement `[LB]`

> **A human seat in this architecture is TAKEN, never imposed; carries NO merit
> definition; requires exactly ONE checkable floor — the signed acknowledgment of
> sub-certainty; and binds its holder to DILIGENCE, never to outcomes the
> architecture itself names unclosable. Four clauses, one grammar: the seat must
> be takeable by an honest party, and its capture must leave symptoms.**

Why one chapter for what could be two: the source already holds the four clauses as
one — the arbiter's posture clause P1 (07b §1) is a single paragraph containing all
four, and 07b §9 names the pair of seats "one sovereignty/qualification refinement."
The fence between 08a and 08b was kept on the same test with opposite evidence; the
test is whether the statements reduce in the source, not whether they rhyme in the
reader.

## 2. The derivational chain — why four clauses are one grammar `[LB]`

The clauses are not a list of virtues; each makes the next necessary. Start from
the design goal: **the recursion terminates in a person (08a §3), so the seat at
the termination point must be one an honest party can actually occupy.**

1. **Diligence, never outcomes.** If the seat carried liability for the residuals
   the architecture itself names unclosable, *"nobody sane accepts liability for
   named-unclosable residuals"* (07a §6) — the seat would select for exactly the
   fools and liars it must exclude. So accountability trims to the work: keep the
   cadence, read the spine, attest at actual sub-certainty, stand in the record.
   *"A doctor cannot be answerable for failing to certify what nothing can
   certify"* — the trim is what makes the seat takeable at all.
2. **Merit deliberately undefined.** With outcomes off the table, the tempting
   substitute is a competence bar. Refused: *"any merit definition is a Goodhart
   target, endlessly gameable, and gamed first by exactly the parties who should
   not hold the seat"* (07a §6; identically 07b §1, P1). A credential is a
   capture-tool with a certificate.
3. **One checkable floor — the sub-certainty acknowledgment.** No merit test
   does not mean no floor. The one predicate the architecture permits itself is
   the one that cannot function as a gameable qualification: the signed
   `subcertainty_acknowledgment` — the holder attests they know the ceiling.
   *"Cheap to sign, and named so: a floor, not a proof"* (07a §6). The
   interpretation ruled by the arbiter (`[TG]` at source, carried as such): the
   *sufficient* qualification is awareness of sub-certainty — no holder is perfect,
   and the one who knows it is the one fit for the seat.
4. **Taken, never imposed.** The chain closes on sovereignty: an imposed doctor
   is *"the imposed-supervisor shape a critique of imposed supervision names self-defeating"*
   (07a §6); acceptance is signed *by* the holder, not *about* them — KNOWN,
   ACCEPTED, TAKEN. This is **offer-not-impose applied at the accountability
   layer itself** — the architecture's founding posture, recursed onto its own
   dependency structure. Withdrawal stays sovereign for the same reason: the
   doctor may lay the role down at any time, typed, without sanction (07a §8).

Remove clause 1 → no honest taker. Remove 2 → the seat pre-captured by
credential-gaming. Remove 3 → no floor at all, the seat un-falsifiably held.
Remove 4 → the supervisor shape, self-defeating. Four clauses, one load path.

## 3. The three seats — one grammar, three registers `[LB]`

**Arbiter** *(the best-tested rendering)*: the posture clause P1 carries all four
clauses in one breath, plus the framing that names what the seat is FOR: kept
*"for being external and unpredictable — accountability, not competence — the
architecture's living 'never 100%'"* (07b §0).

**Doctor** *(the fullest derivation; not yet independently tested, 07a)*: the sovereignty
clause, the Goodhart refusal, the acknowledgment-as-floor, and the
diligence-binding each get their own derivation at 07a §6 — this chapter's chain
in §2 is largely that derivation lifted to seat-generic form. The boundary drawn
there generalizes: maturity, conduct, transparency-in-practice are *"real and
load-bearing in the world, unspecifiable without minting a merit definition"* —
they live as **named dependencies, observable in their symptoms, never
enforced** (07a §7).

**Co-operator** *(the grammar's stress test — a seat that is not even a typed
object yet; its chapter has never been through a review round, and is carried at
that standing)*: the apparent counterexample resolves INTO the grammar. The
competence floor ("no baby at the red button") looks like a merit test and is not:
*"the floor is a named dependency, observable in its symptoms, never an enforced
predicate"* (07c §5) — MICA says out loud that the promise depends on the
receiver's competence and never certifies it; failure shows as symptoms
(click-through cadence, ignored calls, alarm-fatigue signatures). And the diligence
trim reaches even this unformalized seat through the ruling on legal consequence:
MICA has no standing to assign outcome-liability to *any* human node (07c §5). The
grammar holds where there is no record to hold it.

**The resolution worth naming (11 §5, closed in prose at 07c):**
merit-undefined and the competence floor are *"the same boundary"* — one
architectural refusal (no merit-Goodharting) applied to two failure surfaces.
The doctor's rendering of the floor is the signed acknowledgment; the
co-operator's is the named dependency with no typed object — *"which is exactly
the gap named at 07d §3."* `[LB]`

## 4. Why — what the grammar buys `[LB]`

The seats exist to terminate self-certification recursions (08a). But a
termination point is only as good as its occupancy: a seat no honest party can
take gets taken by dishonest ones, and the recursion's corruption re-imports one
level up wearing a human face. The grammar cannot verify the person — interiority
is the residual named in P5 (07b §1) at every seat — so it shapes the *seat*
instead:

- clauses 1+4 select FOR honest takers (takeable, sovereign, diligence-bounded);
- clause 2 removes the credential channel a capturer would game first;
- clause 3 plants the one flag a holder must personally sign — and a signature
  without meaning defeats it *trivially and visibly as that class of residual*,
  not silently as a passed test.

The complement is symptom-visibility, and it is already in canon rather than in
this chapter: gaps, lockstep-vs-divergence patterns, engagement-depth
time-series, random-audit presence-or-absence (07a §4–§5, 07b §4). The grammar
makes capture *cost symptoms*; the record makes symptoms *visible*; what follows
belongs to governance and law (P2/P3, 07b §1). Show-not-enforce (08g), at the seat.

## 5. The principle's own snug bound

- The grammar does **not** claim the seats are safe. The hollow bureaucrat
  (07b §1, P5) passes every clause; engagement-in-substance is unverifiable at
  every seat; the random-audit obligation is *"the live mend, not a closure."*
  The grammar shapes selection pressure and capture-cost; it proves nothing
  about the occupant.
- The sufficient-qualification interpretation (clause 3's "awareness of
  sub-certainty suffices") stays `[TG]` as at source — ruled by the arbiter of
  record, held as interpretation, not hardened here.
- Whether the co-operator seat should ever become a typed object (its canon home ruled by the arbiter: located acknowledgment, not typed object, 07c §8) is untouched by this chapter: the grammar covers it either
  way — as typed seat or as permanent named dependency.
