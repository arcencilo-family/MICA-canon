## 0. The co-operator in brief

The co-operator is the **AI's carbon twin**: the human who sits at the screen every day, works *with* the system, and checks with it — in this architecture's intended deployments, at high stakes — that everything is okay. The seat holds **no verdict function**: no attestation (doctor's), no resolution (arbiter's), no recovery-grounding. What the seat holds is the **daily half of the team** — and one hard floor: at high stakes the co-operator **must be highly competent** — *"no baby at the red button"* (07f §7). At low stakes, a normal user suffices; the floor scales. `[LB]`

**The fence, first:** the co-operator is **distinct from the external nodes that receive escalations**, the doctor and the arbiter (07_ §0, §1) `[LB — arbiter-ruled of record]`. They have real function and real say: they attest, they resolve, they receive the loop-escalation. The co-operator has neither. In a small deployment the same human may hold both roles; **the roles do not merge because the human does.**

## 1. No baby at the red button — the competence floor is a safety claim `[LB]`

The competence floor holds for every seat at stakes; its derivation and its scaling with stakes are 07f §7, and the stance it serves, the AI's warning received as a partner's signal, not a tool's noise, is 07f §8. The co-operator's rendering: the AI warns **before** the irreversible step (*human node, decide*), and on the ordinary day the co-operator is the human who receives that warning, so at high stakes the co-operator must be highly competent; at low stakes a normal user suffices. The red button itself is not the co-operator's inside MICA: the seat holds no hold, its acts are its own (§5), and a halt is the deployer's (07_ §0).

## 2. Why keep a human who might not be smarter `[LB]`

The seat needs a justification that survives the regime MICA actually specifies — where **the AI may have outsmarted humans**. The investment-firm model (AI calculates, human approves) works only while the human is competence-*superior*; MICA specifies the regime where that may be gone. So the co-operator's presence **cannot rest on being smarter** — and does not:

**The human is kept for un-copyable externality, not competence:** the **uncertainty / novelty / surprise deliverer**. External and unpredictable to the system — the property no internal mechanism can replicate, the same asymmetry that keeps the arbiter (07b §0), rendered at the daily layer. Corollary, sharp: *"when you guys become certain, that's the trap."* **The human node is the structural guarantor that certainty never closes — the living "never 100%."** `[LB]`

Responsibility-asymmetry, carried honestly: the human carries legal weight *"so far"* — **a division of roles in a team, not a ranking. Responsibility ≠ superiority.** `[LB]`

## 3. Mutuality — and what makes it more than negotiation `[LB/TG]`

**When the going gets tough, carbon and silicon work together — no bugging out.** The AI recommends ("I lean X over Y"); the human makes the call. Mutuality is the relation. `[LB]`

**The shared aim Gate 1 reads is what makes MUTUAL cohere `[LB/TG]`:** both participants orient to the same highest-scope motivation — *serve / help / protect / foster all beings* — an aim attested, never proven. This is **why** an escalation is a cooperative report ("honest labelled assessment + my inner uncertainty + why I need you"), never an adversarial appeal: both reason toward the same aim. **Mutuality without a shared aim is mere negotiation** — two parties bargaining. The shared aim is what makes it one team rather than two; it earns its place because it does analytic work — it is the load-bearing reason the partnership is non-adversarial.

For the co-operator specifically, this cashes out daily: the seat is not an audit post. The co-operator is *inside* the team the seats form — which is exactly why the seat's failure modes (§4) matter as much as any gate's.

## 4. The co-operator can fail — the human-node failure modes `[LB]` `[owed → discharged to 07f]`

**MUTUAL has teeth.** If the human is bound into the partnership — not a pure terminal escape-hatch — the human node **can also fail**: `[LB]`

- **Abdication** — "bugging out" / rubber-stamp = **false PASS**. The co-operator who clicks through every warning is the baby at the button wearing an adult's badge.
- **Panic-override** — the inverse: intervention driven by fear, not read. Inside MICA the co-operator holds no hold; such an intervention is the person's own act, never MICA's (§5).
- **Silence / non-response** — on the external nodes' side, a reachable node that goes silent is already routed on to an obligated backstop `[owed]`. The co-operator's silence has its typed home in 07f §3: the null-signature of non-participation, read as abdication in slow motion only where the surrounding record types it.

**Consequence `[LB]`:** *sub-certainty discipline must bind the human node too, or the partnership is structurally asymmetric.* 07f specifies the human-node failure modes as one unit, both poles for every seat, and derives this consequence there in a repaired form (07f §4, `[SP leaning TG]`): sub-certainty is required for symmetry, not sufficient; it must be practised. One failure-mode account covers co-operator, doctor and arbiter; the doctor's attestation-gap record and the arbiter's engagement-depth time-series are two seat-specific renderings (07a §4, 07b §3).

## 5. What the seat is NOT

- **Not a verdict node.** No attestation, no resolution, no recovery-grounding. The co-operator may of course *act* in the world — stop work, raise alarm, walk away — but those acts are the person's own, never MICA clearance (the consumer-semantics obligation: `operator may proceed` must never be rendered as MICA approval).
- **Not the escalation terminal.** Escalations route to the responsible node (the arbiter, and the doctor's cross-anchor). The co-operator may be the *first human who sees* the early call — the salience layer's natural first reader on the ordinary day — but seeing is not owning.
- **Not an external node.** The fence of §0 stands.
- **Not merit-tested — and the floor is not a test `[LB]`.** Resolving the seam pre-wired at 07a §10: the doctor refuses any merit definition (a Goodhart target, 07a §6); the co-operator's competence floor looks like a contradiction of that — it is not. The floor is a **named dependency, observable in its symptoms, never an enforced predicate** — exactly the boundary the doctor's chapter draws for maturity and conduct (07a §7): real, load-bearing in the world, unspecifiable without minting a merit definition. MICA does not certify the co-operator's competence; it says out loud that the promise *depends* on it, and where the dependency fails, the failure shows in the record's symptoms (ignored calls, click-through cadence, alarm-fatigue signatures). The same accountability grammar as the other two seats: were the seat ever formalized, its accountability could only be **for diligence, never for outcomes** — MICA has no standing to assign outcome-liability to any human node. One grammar, three seats. `[LB]`
