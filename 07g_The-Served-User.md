# Chapter 07g — The served user

**Covers:** the person a MICA deployment serves: who they are, what MICA owes them, what they can offer, the recommended practice around them, and the open edges.
**Status:** A proxy paper: untested, offered for review; speculative as a whole.
**Source:** 07_ §0 and §3 and 08o §3.
Claim tags: see 01.

---

## 0. In brief

This whole chapter is speculative `[SP]`: a proxy paper, written for review before anything in it is built or tested. It speaks for a person who holds no seat, so that the record has a place for them. Nothing here is built; what the machine implements today: README and CODE-MAP.

---

## 1. Who

The served user is the person the system serves. The served user holds no seat. One human may also hold a seat; the roles do not merge because the human does (07_ §1).

---

## 2. What MICA owes the served user

- **A contestable finding.** An adverse finding that touches the served user is contestable (08o §3).
- **The stop rule.** An automatic stop that affects the served user is a typed record, with a reachable path to a human and a declared bound on the wait (07_ §3).
- **No claim about who they are.** The record says what it shows, never who the user is.

---

## 3. What the served user can offer

The served user is often the first to notice. A user channel takes reports of harm or error. A report is kept as a logged contact: never a seat, and never a verdict.

---

## 4. Recommended practice, not a rule

- The user is told that the system runs under MICA.
- The fast lane and compensation (07_ §3).
- A report that proves real may be rewarded with a small gift, as a bug bounty rewards a found bug. A reward is for a confirmed report, never for a favourable rating: paying for praise buys noise.
