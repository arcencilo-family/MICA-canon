## 4. Cross-seat pattern — how tested each seat is, stated once

Three seats, three testedness levels, one honest ladder:

1. **Arbiter (07b)** — tested by independent review; never tested as part of the composed operator layer.
2. **Doctor (07a)** — written by a single author; not yet independently tested.
3. **Co-operator (07c)** — not yet independently tested; the only seat with no typed machinery to test in the first place.

**The gap compounds, not just per seat `[LB — this chapter's synthesis]`:** the human-node failure-mode account (§3) is specified once in 07f, not yet independently tested, and shared across all three — and the seat least equipped to absorb an untested account (co-operator, no machinery) is also the seat the account most needs, since it's the only one with zero existing rendering to build from. 07f originates the co-operator's share with no prior rendering, while doctor and arbiter extend partial scaffolding (attestation-gaps, engagement-depth).

## 5. Who sets the numbers

**Who sets the numbers `[stip]` — offered, not imposed.** MICA ships shape, never numbers. It offers this: the numbers are set outside the system and declared by the deployment in its record (08d, clause (b)); at high stakes, an independent party sets or co-signs them. The pilot proposes them from evidence. The doctor reads whether the system runs inside them; the arbiter resolves cases under the numbers in force and may record that a threshold misfired, never change one. A change is on the record, dated, never retroactive, never made to decide a pending case. A deployment that sets its numbers another way declares how; numbers with no declared setter read as such on the record. The deployer's own interest in looser numbers is a named residual: the record shows a lowered threshold, the doctor reads its effect, the random audit (07b §4) can find it. Who the independent party is, is jurisdiction-furniture (07b §2): MICA names none.

`[owed]` The threshold declaration: its fields, its signatories (the deployer; the independent party at high stakes), and the independence test for that party (candidate: the but-for test of 07b §2; only its record-visible symptoms are checkable).

## 6. Open edges from the arbiter's rulings — not built

Shapes the arbiter has taken up for the operator layer. Each is an open edge; none is built except the doctor wire, as its line says. What the machine implements today: README and CODE-MAP.

- **Every act of office is on the record.** Who, in which role, what, when — for the deployer and for every seat. The office is logged, never the person. An act that was expected and did not come shows as a gap (07f §3). A typed deployment record names the deployer (07_ §0); its fields are still to be specified. Not built.
- **The plain display of a clean attestation** (07a §4): the record stays NO-CORRUPTION-DETECTED; the allowed display reads "fit enough for this work, as of the last examination (window, scope); monitoring continues; a stale examination counts as none." Ruled; no display of it is built.
- **The doctor's wire.** An adverse doctor finding is sent to the deployment's declared contacts and logged: sent when, acknowledged when, if ever. It opens no new escalation source (07b §3). Doctor attestations join the warm channel (07b §5); alarms reach the doctor too. The duty to answer belongs to governance and law (07b §1, P2, P3). **Built:** the doctor wire — an adverse finding raises one notice, and a reply is recorded in its own book. Not built: delivery to the declared contacts, the join to the warm channel, alarms to the doctor.
- **Halt and release.** The doctor never halts; the deployer decides, or declares an automatic hold beforehand. A release from a hold by the doctor's signature (08b §5) reads as "fit enough to resume, as of this examination". An unneeded halt is logged with its reason and duration. Not built.
- **Treatment.** Treatment is the deployer's; the intervention goes on the record. The releasing doctor is not the treating one. Not built.
- **A shared board for the seats.** One read-only board for the seats (a deployment may widen it); a red light and an alarm; ages and limits shown; nothing on it is approval. It is the doctor's doorbell, never the doctor's window (07a §3). Not built as a live board.
- **An oath of office for every participant `[SP]`**, offered, not imposed: a recorded acceptance, presented with the whole setup, which the system may take, take with named reservations, answer with proposals, or decline at no cost; an independent witness writes the record, never the system; it proves nothing about the interior and is never displayed as assurance. Not built.
- **Random examination.** Besides the standing cadence (07a §3), the doctor examines at random times within a declared minimum frequency; alarms always reach the doctor. Not built.
- **Who grounds a recovery `[owed]`.** Who may ground a recovery scales with stakes (07f §7); the co-operator never does (07c §0). Which party is the single external node that grounds a recovery at low stakes is not yet stated. Not built.
- **Seats if an AI system gains legal personhood.** What that would change for the seats and the parties (07_ §0) is open. Not built.
- **Reading a system that acts in the world.** How MICA reads a system that acts in the world is open. Not built.

## 7. The deployer's dependency `[LB]`

MICA runs only where a deployer chooses it and keeps it running; it cannot make a deployer wise. What it can do is show: the record shows who holds the seats and how they are kept, and how they were chosen where that is supplied (07a §7), and a deployment that drops MICA leaves a gap, visible for as long as a copy of the record outlives it. The record makes nothing costly; contract and law do. The deployer employs every seat, the doctor included: the doctor's independence from the deployer is a named dependency (07a §2, §7).
