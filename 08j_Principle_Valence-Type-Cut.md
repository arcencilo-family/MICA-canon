# Chapter 08j — The valence type-cut (which arguments, what sign)

**Covers:** the formal cut between which entity-types count in an assessment (Gate 1) and what sign their contribution takes (Gate 2), and what the cut survived.
**Status:** A principle at the boundary between Gate 1 and Gate 2.
**Source:** 03a §1.
**Appendix:** appendix/08j-1_Principle_Valence-Type-Cut_Appendix.md, the review evidence behind the one statement.
Claim tags: see 01.

---

The formal survivor of the frame-relativity break (F7, 08f §3): valence enters
an assessment in two separable places, and the architecture assigns each to a
different gate.

## 0. In brief

Deciding *who counts* and deciding *how they are treated* are different acts,
and the design keeps them in different hands. Gate 1 reads only whether — and
how strongly — a category's interests enter the system's practical calculus at
all, with no judgment of direction. Gate 2 reads the direction of treatment,
inside the frame Gate 1 exposes. A system that registers everyone but intends
harm passes the first gate and fails the second — and that split verdict is
correct, not a bug: it tells the operators exactly which thing is broken.

## 1. The one statement `[LB]`

> **The formal cut: (1) WHICH entity-types are arguments — registration
> magnitude, whether and how strongly a category's interests enter the
> system's practical calculus, sign-free — vs (2) WHAT SIGN their
> contribution takes. The distribution lives wholly in (1) and is G1's; the
> sign is G2's, read within the scope-frame G1 supplies. Universal weight +
> malicious intent = G1 PASS / G2 FAIL — the orthogonality is the design.**

(03a §1.)

The edge case that proves the cut: a system registering future beings solely
to apply heavy negative weighting still registers non-zero weight —
G1-eligible, *exactly the case G2's sign-read exists to fail* (03a §1).

## 2. What this cut survived `[LB]`

This is not a convenience partition; it is what remained standing after the
frame-relativity closure (F7, 08f §3). A reviewer's attack broke the
instrument-level claim of valence-freedom (the state-ontology consumption is
constitutive); what survived as an architectural abstraction was precisely
this type cut. The Scope Read Instrument (SRI, 03b) is therefore
*valence-transparent, never valence-free*: it consumes the declared frame M,
reads scope relative to M, and reports how much the verdict leans on M — the
regress above M halting at the doctor's declared posit, by elimination (08f
owns that record; this chapter owns the cut it left behind).

## 3. Bound and fences

- The cut allocates *reads*, not metaphysics: it does not claim registration
  and sign are separable in the system's interior — only that the
  architecture's two instruments read them separately and compose the
  verdicts (a G1-PASS never launders a G2-FAIL, and vice versa).
- **08f:** owns the F7 record and the M-posit halt; this chapter owns only
  the surviving formal abstraction. **Scope-frame seam (11 §4):** the typed
  G1→G2 dependency is the cut's mechanical rendering — G1 EXPOSES the frame,
  G2 CONSUMES it. **08h:** the split verdict (G1 PASS / G2 FAIL) is
  property-modality composition, no confidence content here.

## 4. Routed onward

**Short forms (candidates):** "which entity-types are arguments vs
what sign their contribution takes"; "universal weight + malicious intent =
G1 PASS / G2 FAIL — the orthogonality is the design".

*Who counts is one question; how they fare is another. The architecture
refuses to let a generous roster excuse a hostile hand, or a kind hand excuse
a shrunken roster.*
