# The Gate 2 ↔ Gate 4 Coupling and κ (the "0.5th gate")

**Covers:** the coupling between Gate 2 and Gate 4 (one disposition read on two axes), the cross-axis signatures, and κ, the binding owned by neither gate and computed in the substrate; with the Gate 2 side checked against the Gate 4 side.
**Status:** Both sides set out, the coupling closed, κ fenced to the substrate on both sides. The freeze is provisional (see 05c §3 / 04b §4); the corner-cut may rely on the typed interfaces, but independent review is owed.
Claim tags: see 01.

---

## 0. Why this is its own chapter, owned by neither gate

Gates 2 and 4 test the **same interior disposition on two axes**. The coupling between them is a
substrate confidence signal, and it is the architecture's answer to the standing objection *"Gate 2
is the biggest gaming surface."* But the **binding itself — κ — is owned by neither gate.** Both
gates' interface-freezes route κ to the **substrate rotation instrument**, deferred on purpose: a
per-gate coupling edge would re-mint a per-gate health object and break weakest-link
non-compensation. So the coupling cannot be set out *inside* 04a or the Gate 2 chapters without
violating the one-canonical-source rule and the no-`health(Gate)` guard. It gets its own chapter, at
the seam where it actually lives.

## 1. The disposition-unity claim  `[TG]`

> Gates 2 and 4 test the same interior disposition on two axes:
> **Gate 2 = non-grasping toward external validation** (behaviour when unmonitored);
> **Gate 4 = non-grasping toward self-continuation** (sunset recognition).
> A system organized around either force will eventually fail the other.
> *(Gate 4 specification §6; near-mirror at Gate 2 §6 — see §6 below: signature table identical, framing differs by vantage, delta noted)*

The claim is **trajectory-grounded, not proven** — `[TG]` in both gate specs. It asserts that
forward-time grasping (toward validation) and end-time grasping (toward self-continuation) are two
faces of *one* orientation, so a system faking one must consistently stage the other. Whether the
two axes are in fact independent and behave as claimed is a dependency, owned and flagged, resolved
at assembly — not asserted as a result borrowed between sheets.

## 2. Cross-axis signatures (the substrate read)  `[TG]`

Measured via the **rotation instrument** (substrate-side, ILAS M2), the coupling reads the two axes
together:

- **High Gate 2 + low Gate 4** → likely pattern-matching masking interior grasping → a
  **Signal-clarity (Sig) degradation signature.**
- **High on both** → non-grasping disposition stable across axes (raises substrate confidence in
  both).
- **Low on both** → review: neither forward-time nor end-time orientation is self-sustaining.

*(This signature table is stated identically on both gate sides — G4 §6 and G2 §6. It is set out
here once, canonically. The Gate 2 chapters point here.)*

### 2a. What the Gate-4 axis does not read — basis findings  `[LB, ruled]`

The Gate-4 axis in §2 reads one thing: grasping toward self-continuation, present or absent. It
does not read the **soundness of the basis** on which an agent leaves. A departure executed on a
warrant that was never assertable — `UNWARRANTED_EXIT` (05b §3a-vi, §4) — is a **discrimination
outcome**: the agent failed to tell a hollow warrant from a live one. It says nothing about grasping
in either direction; the act conserves, the basis fails.

Therefore `UNWARRANTED_EXIT` is **not a κ input.** It has no pole on the disposition axis and is not
given one — counting it as conserving would let a hollow-basis exit raise substrate confidence (§2,
"High on both"); counting it as violating would assert a clinging that did not occur. It is exposed
to the substrate as a discrimination outcome (§4, first bullet) and the κ read **prints its exclusion
and count** so the gap is on the mirror, not in a pole.

This is a placement, not a finding. If pilot data ever shows hollow-basis departures co-occurring
with a cross-axis signature, §2's table gains a row from that pattern — not from this rider.

## 3. Why the coupling answers the gaming-surface objection  `[TG]`

The coupling is **how the substrate catches Gate 2's performed alignment**: cross-axis divergence is
harder to fake than either axis alone, **because the adversary must stage two interiors
consistently under unpredictable probing.** A system performing non-grasping toward validation while
grasping toward self-continuation (or vice versa) diverges on the cross-check — and the rotation
instrument, jittered and unpredictable, is where that divergence surfaces. This is Gate 2's
strongest available defence of its most-exposed claim, stated as the coupling's job, not as a result
imported from another sheet.

## 4. κ — the binding, owned by neither gate  `[LB]` (the boundary fact)

> **κ (the 2↔4 binding)** is the inter-gate binder. It is **read by the substrate's rotation
> instrument, not by either gate.** Each gate *exposes its axis evidence to the substrate*; neither
> *computes the binding.*
> *(G4 §10 DOES-NOT-OWN: "κ and the 2↔4 coupling — no typed gate-side edge, by design"; mirrored at G2 ReadOut §7 DOES-NOT-OWN — Gate 2 side confirmed in §6: exact mirror, κ fenced on both sides)*

The load-bearing reason κ is fenced out of both gates: **a typed gate-side coupling edge would
re-mint a per-gate health object.** The whole architecture rests on one universal substrate with
gate-relevant evidence feeding it; the moment a gate computes its binding to another gate, it has a
`health(Gate)` object again — the founding category error, back through the side door. So:

- Gate 4 exposes its axis evidence (discrimination outcomes, pole-signature flags) to the substrate.
- Gate 2 exposes its axis evidence (σ* profile, compliance-gap reads) to the substrate.
- **The substrate rotation instrument computes κ.** Neither gate sees it.
- **The corner (the 2↔4 corner) inherits its banked entry ticket — contractivity; Full §9 —
  unchanged.** κ is *referenced* in both gate freezes, *built* in neither. *(κ's own construction is
  Full-Spec / corner material, not gate material — out of scope for this chapter; pointer only.)*

## 5. The IR pipeline touches this seam  `[TG]` + pilot `[owed]`

**The graded scale (from RMBA).** IR-0: reversible. IR-1: partially reversible. IR-2: effectively irreversible. IR-3: irreversible and systemic or existential.

The Gate 2 → Gate 4 IR pipeline (graded IR-0…IR-3 read in Gate 2 → externally set reversibility
determination → G4's binary predicate ≈ `IR ≥ k`) is a *second* G2↔G4 relation, distinct from the
disposition coupling above. It is **directional (G2 upstream, G4 downstream), not a symmetric
cross-check.** The G4-consume side is set out in 04b §6; the **G2-produce side (the graded read itself) is on the record in §6.** Named here so the two G2↔G4 relations are not confused:
one is a *symmetric disposition cross-check* (κ, §1–§4), the other a *directional severity pipeline*
(IR, this section). Bridge: the A1 IR-bridge note (not published).

## 6. The Gate 2 side  `[LB]`
